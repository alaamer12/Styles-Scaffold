What you'll see

Four places set the shape of the week. Everything else hangs off them.

Fushimi Inari at first light

Ten thousand vermilion gates climb a mountain. We start at 06:00, before the first bus arrives, and walk it upward while it is still silent.

The Sagano bamboo grove

A windless day in Arashiyama still moves — the whole canopy knocks overhead. We go late afternoon, then tea at a teahouse at the edge of the grove.

Kinkaku-ji, the golden pavilion

Two of its three storeys are covered in gold leaf, and the pond was designed to give the building a second, steadier copy.

Gion after dark

The lanterns come on around 17:40 and the teahouse lanes change character completely. We walk them slowly, and then eat.

The seven days

Each morning is planned; each afternoon leaves room. Nothing starts before the group has had coffee.

Day one

Arrival, and the station roof

Meet at Kyoto Station, drop bags, then walk the old south side. Easy evening — Hirano shrine, and dinner wherever the group feels like stopping.

Day two

Higashiyama on foot

Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka before the shops open, Kiyomizu-dera from the rear approach, then down to Gion for the evening walk.

Day three

Inari at 06:00

The full climb while the gates are empty. Afternoon at leisure — the museum row, or a second attempt on the mountain if your legs allow.

Day four

North-west, and a rock garden

Kinkaku-ji early, then Ryoan-ji's fifteen stones and the Nyojindō pavilion. This is the day the guide explains what a garden is actually doing.

Day five

Arashiyama and the bamboo

Tenryū-ji garden in the morning, the grove and Okochi villa after, a boat down the Hozugawa if the water is high enough.

Day six

Day trip, and a ryokan night

Train to Nara for the hall at Tōdai-ji, back by late afternoon, then north into the Higashiyama hills — the machiya, and kaiseki dinner served in your room.

Day seven

Maples, market, departure

Ginkaku-ji's moss garden first thing, the Nishiki market for anything you want to take home, and the station by two o'clock.

Questions

How much walking is involved?

Between six and nine kilometres a day, most of it on flat paved lane. Day three includes the Inari climb — about four hundred metres of steady steps, and you can stop at the Yotsutsuji viewpoint halfway instead of continuing.

Is the April departure before the maple colour?

Yes. April is cherry blossom, not momiji. The maple photographs on this page are from the November departure. If maples are the reason you are coming, book November 8.

What is included in the price?

Seven nights' accommodation, all temple and garden admissions, guiding, and dinner on six of the seven evenings. International flights, travel insurance and lunch are not included.

Can I do this alone?

Roughly half of each group travels alone. Solo travellers share a twin room and pay the listed price; a private room is an extra ¥34,000 for the week.
